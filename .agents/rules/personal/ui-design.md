# UI design

When creating or changing UI:

- Follow the design patterns already in place. Do not invent new patterns in existing projects unless the operator explicitly requests a departure.
- Assume users do not read. Show, don't tell: make behavior and state evident in the interface instead of relying on explanatory copy.
- Remove nonfunctional copy. Text must help users identify, decide, act, or recover; remove filler, redundant explanations, and decorative prose. Keep necessary labels and accessible names.
- Use 2-3 font styles at most unless the existing design system offers more.
- Establish a clear information hierarchy. Users must be able to find what is relevant quickly.
- Design for skimming. Avoid clutter and keep related elements visually cohesive.
- Less is more. Do not add components, controls, or decoration merely because they are available.
- Make the most important action obvious.
- Keep rare and unimportant actions less prominent and out of the way.
- Match the space to the task. Do not cram complex interactions into tight modals or stretch small features across oversized spaces.
